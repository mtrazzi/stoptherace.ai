On Anthropic’s IPO Day, Protesters Demand a Ban on Recursive Self‑Improvement

Anthropic’s own prospectus warns that catastrophic and existential risks “will increase as AI systems substantially automate AI research and development.”

San Francisco · November 19, 2026

On Thursday, November 19, the day of Anthropic’s IPO, over 200 protesters rallied outside the company’s headquarters at 500 Howard Street in San Francisco with one demand: ban recursive self-improvement (RSI), the use of AI systems to do the work of building more powerful AI. Page 32 of Anthropic’s own prospectus discusses concerns about “catastrophic or existential risks to humanity.”

Speakers included Daniel Kokotajlo, the former OpenAI researcher and co-author of AI 2027, who left OpenAI over safety concerns and spoke outside his former employer for the first time at the March for an AI Treaty on November 14; Jacob Coxon; AI safety YouTuber Robert Miles; David Krueger of Evitable; and Michael Trazzi of Stop the AI Race.

Anthropic tells investors that using Claude to build Claude is central to its business. “Each version of a Claude model assists in building the next,” the prospectus says, calling it “one of the most significant structural advantages in frontier intelligence.” The same filing acknowledges that “using AI to help build more capable AI could also pose a considerable safety risk.”

From Anthropic’s IPO prospectus

Page 7 The pitch

“Each version of a Claude model assists in building the next, helping to compress development timelines […] we believe it is one of the most significant structural advantages in frontier intelligence.” sec.gov →

Page 32 The risk

“Concerns have been raised […] within our organization […] that sufficiently advanced AI systems could […] pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity […] these risks will increase as AI systems substantially automate AI research and development.” sec.gov →

Page 193 The plan

“Using AI to help build more capable AI could also pose a considerable safety risk […] we are considering updates to our Responsible Scaling Policy to keep humans in control of frontier model development.” sec.gov →

Protesters say the line should be drawn at today’s level: AI already helps build AI, but nothing stronger than current systems should be put to work building its successor. Without that line, they argue, AI development will keep getting faster until it reaches the limits of intelligence, leaving humanity no time to deal with the risks and no human in the loop to understand whether the systems are safe.

The rally asked Anthropic to announce a plan to refrain from recursive self-improvement, and to urge other AI companies to follow suit or lobby for legislation that bans it. Organizers chose Anthropic because, they say, it has invested the most in RSI and pushed other companies to optimize for it, and because the IPO, which finances that work, is the moment the public conversation about AI turns to profitability rather than danger.
